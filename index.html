    Jump to a question
    The three that decide most math questions: (1) Are you multiplying or dividing? Going backwards always means divide. (2) Is the base the price or the loan? (3) Does the seller own the closing day?

    Minnesota transfer taxes — memorize cold

    State deed tax — price × 0.0033
    Paid by the seller, based on the sale price (net consideration). Hennepin & Ramsey add a 0.0001 ERF tax → 0.0034. Consideration of $3,000 or less = $1.65 flat.
    Mortgage registry tax — loan × 0.0023
    Paid by the buyer, based on the principal debt. Hennepin & Ramsey add 0.0001 → 0.0024.

    Commission

    Total commission = sale price × rate
    Then apply the brokerage split, then the agent's split. One step at a time, outside in.
    Net to seller — price = (net wanted + costs) ÷ (1 − rate)
    Divide, never add the percentage to the net. This is the single most-missed math question.
    Working backwards from a commission check
    Divide by the agent split, divide by the brokerage split, divide by the rate.

    Prorations

    daily rate = annual amount ÷ 360 or 365
    Read which year the question wants. 360-day years use 30-day months.
    Direction of the entry
    Paid in arrears (Minnesota property taxes): seller owes the buyer → debit seller, credit buyer.
    Prepaid (insurance, HOA dues, rent already collected): → credit seller, debit buyer — except rent, where the seller collected money for days the buyer will own, so the buyer gets the credit.
    Who owns the closing day?
    The question always tells you. It moves the answer by exactly one day, and the off-by-one answer is always offered.

    Financing

    loan = value × LTV   down = price − loan
    Lenders always use the lesser of sale price or appraised value.
    Discount points — 1 point = 1% of the LOAN
    Never a percentage of the sale price.
    Interest — I = P × R × T
    Per diem = annual interest ÷ 365 (or 360). Mortgage interest is paid in arrears.
    Amortization, one month at a time
    interest = balance × rate ÷ 12, then principal = payment − interest, then subtract from the balance and repeat.
    Payment from a factor — (loan ÷ 1,000) × factor
    Divide by 1,000 first.
    Qualifying ratios 28 / 36
    Front-end = income × 28%. Back-end = (income × 36%) − other monthly debts. The buyer qualifies for the lower of the two.
    PITI = P&I + taxes/12 + insurance/12 + PMI/12
    Convert every annual figure to monthly before adding.
    equity = current market value − all liens
    Current value, not the purchase price. Subtract every lien, not just the first mortgage.

    Valuation & investment

    IRV — Income = Rate × Value
    So Value = NOI ÷ cap rate and cap rate = NOI ÷ value. NOI is calculated before debt service — never subtract the mortgage payment.
    Getting to NOI
    Potential gross − vacancy = effective gross; effective gross − operating expenses = NOI.
    GRM — price ÷ monthly gross rent
    Monthly GRM runs roughly 100–200; an annual multiplier (GIM) runs roughly 6–12. Check which one is given.
    Cost approach — cost new − depreciation + land
    Straight-line depreciation = effective age ÷ economic life. Land never depreciates.
    Percent change — (new − old) ÷ OLD
    To find the original price from a price that includes a gain, divide by (1 + gain).
    Capital gain
    Adjusted basis = purchase + improvements. Gain = (sale price − selling costs) − adjusted basis.
    Property tax — (assessed value ÷ 1,000) × mills
    1 mill = $1 per $1,000 = 0.001. (Minnesota really uses a tax-capacity system, but the national portion tests mills.)

    Area & land

    1 acre = 43,560 sq ft
    Square feet → acres: divide. Acres → square feet: multiply.
    1 section = 640 acres = 1 square mile
    36 sections = 1 township. Quarter section = 160 acres. Quarter-quarter = 40 acres. For a legal description, multiply all the fractions together and apply to 640.
    Other conversions
    1 mile = 5,280 ft  •  1 sq yard = 9 sq ft  •  triangle = ½ × base × height
    Front foot
    Priced per foot of street frontage. The depth given in the problem is a deliberate distractor.

    Closing statement direction

    Debit = you owe it. Credit = you already have it or it's given to you.
    Sale price: debit buyer, credit seller. New loan and earnest money: credit buyer. Seller's loan payoff and the deed tax: debit seller.
    Buyer's cash to close
    (price + closing costs + prepaids) − (loan + earnest money + credits owed by seller).

    Minnesota facts that show up as numbers

    Contract for deed cancellation (Minn. Stat. 559.21)
    Under 10% paid → 30 days to cure  •  10% to under 25% → 60 days  •  25% or more → 90 days.
    Foreclosure
    Minnesota is a lien theory state. Standard redemption period after a sheriff's sale is 6 months.
    Trust account
    If the agreement is silent, earnest money goes in within 3 business days of receipt or final acceptance, whichever is later. A rejected offer's earnest money returns by the next business day.
    Licensing
    Pre-license: 90 hours (three 30-hour courses; Course I before the exam). CE: 30 hours per 2-year cycle, at least 15 each year by June 30, including the required module.
    New-home statutory warranty (Ch. 327A)
    1 year workmanship & materials  •  2 years plumbing / electrical / heating / cooling  •  10 years major structural.
    Other deadlines
    Security deposit returned within 21 days  •  MCIOA resale cancellation within 10 days of the disclosure certificate  •  lead-based paint (pre-1978) inspection opportunity 10 days.

    Fair housing classes

    Federal — 7 classes
    Race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, disability.
    Minnesota Human Rights Act adds
    Creed, marital status, status with regard to public assistance, sexual orientation and gender identity, and age in some contexts. Minnesota protects more than the federal law does — the state portion tests this.

    Minnesota vs. North Dakota — quick contrast

    Parker's exam is the Minnesota one. This is only so the two do not blur together later when he sits for the ND license.

    Regulator
    MN: Commissioner of Commerce (Dept. of Commerce), Minn. Stat. Ch. 82.
    ND: North Dakota Real Estate Commission, N.D.C.C. Ch. 43-23.
    Non-agent status
    MN formally recognizes the facilitator. Do not assume the same label or duties apply in ND.
    Transfer tax
    MN charges deed tax and mortgage registry tax. North Dakota has no real estate transfer tax — a classic mix-up for border agents.
    Practice note
    A license is state-specific. Property in Moorhead is a Minnesota transaction under Minnesota law even if the buyer, the brokerage, and the lender are all in Fargo.
